How foraging works

Every trip into the bush is a handful of rolls. Each roll is like pulling one thing out of a big bag. What's in the bag, and how much of each, comes from the forage table.

Weights

Each line in the table has a weight. Bigger weight, more of the bag. If leaves have weight 30 and truffles 2, a roll is fifteen times more likely to find leaves than a truffle. The chance of one roll finding something is its weight divided by the total weight of everything in the bag.

Each line also has a min and max (how many you get when it's picked), a time (Any, Day only or Night only) and sometimes a quest it needs: the first recipe scrap only drops while The lost recipe is active. Items marked unique drop once ever. Once the player has one, it leaves the bag for good.

Modifiers

Companions and perks carry modifiers. The focus you pick becomes a weight boost for each of its tags.

  • TagWeight: items with this tag get their weight multiplied (Kea: bark ×3).
  • TagQuantity: every find of an item with this tag gives extra (+1 bark per find).
  • RarityWeight: items of this rarity get their weight multiplied (Rare ×2).
  • ExtraRolls: more rolls per trip (Big basket: +1 find).
  • BonusRoll: after the normal rolls, a chance of one extra roll that can only find items with this tag (Weka: 40% chance to snatch something shiny).

Weight boosts multiply together, so a ×2 focus on top of a ×2.5 companion makes ×5. Extra quantities and extra rolls round down, so a value of 1.7 counts as 1.

Seeds

The dice are a seeded random number generator. The same design data, setup and seed always give the same trip, here and in Unity. When something odd happens, note the seed on the result ("seed 12345 gave 3 truffles") and anyone can replay it exactly.

Reading the odds

  • Chance per roll: how likely one roll is to find it.
  • At least one per trip: how likely a whole trip is to find one or more. It ignores bonus rolls, so it's a slight underestimate.
  • Expected per trip: the average amount you'd bring home per trip over many trips (bonus rolls included).

The Simulator runs thousands of trips for exact averages, and answers the design goal: after one or two trips, can the player cook a recipe and finish a quest?
